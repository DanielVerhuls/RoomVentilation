    Simulation

    In a real room, hobs, showers and people drive a warm, rising circulation that mixes the air within minutes. A top view has no buoyancy, so that mixing is set here as a turbulent diffusivity.

    Things to try

    • Close the door: the wind-driven cross flow stops at once, because the window alone has nothing to push against. The ventilation units keep running.
    • Turn the wind to 0° (from north): the window ends up on the leeward side, under suction, and the flow reverses.
    • CO₂: with everything closed it rises steadily. Compare the room mean with the well-mixed steady state C = Cin + G/Q; that's what a ventilation engineer would compute by hand.
    • Bathroom: let the shower run, watch the humidity and the condensate grow, then switch the shower off and see how long the extract fan needs. Try the door open vs. closed (the 1 cm gap under it feeds the fan).
    • Kitchen: switch the hood off while frying potatoes and watch the fumes. With the hood on, see where its replacement air comes from - and how cold the kitchen gets in winter.
    • Temperature only goes along for the ride here: in a top view warm air cannot rise, so heat never drives the flow.
    • max |div u| is mass conservation after the pressure projection; it should stay near 1e-14.
